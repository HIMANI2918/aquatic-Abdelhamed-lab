Research program

A One Health Program to Combat Antimicrobial Resistance

From AMR emergence and transmission to intervention, stewardship, and AI-enabled prevention.

1

Define the Drivers of AMR Emergence & Transmission

Determine how coinfection, antimicrobial exposure, and environmental pressures promote AMR emergence, persistence, and horizontal gene transfer.

2

Identify Environmental Reservoirs & Risk Factors

Define how host, microbial, and environmental factors contribute to the dissemination of AMR.

Host • Microbe • Environment

3

AI-Enabled Early Disease Detection & AMR Prevention

Use deep-learning behavioral monitoring to detect disease earlier, enable timely intervention, and potentially reduce unnecessary antimicrobial use.

Early detection • Timely intervention • Prevention

4

Develop Strategies to Reduce Antimicrobial Use & AMR Spread

Translate research findings into evidence-based interventions, education, and stewardship that reduce AMR.

Intervention • Education • Stewardship

Medical relevance: understand how resistance emerges and spreads, then translate that knowledge into strategies that reduce antimicrobial selection pressure.

Research Highlights

Understanding AMR emergence and spread

What is the mechanism of resistance?

  • Resistance in aquaculture pathogens is mediated by plasmids.
  • Multidrug-resistance (MDR) plasmids can transfer among other bacterial species.
  • Plasmids are highly stable in the absence of selective pressure.

Genetic & environmental drivers of AMR

  • Identify factors influencing MDR plasmid stability and transfer.
  • Study conducted under realistic catfish farm conditions.
  • Results provide a mechanistic understanding of AMR persistence.

Risk prediction & decision support

  • Develop predictive models of AMR emergence and spread.
  • Build decision-support tools for farm-level AMR management.
